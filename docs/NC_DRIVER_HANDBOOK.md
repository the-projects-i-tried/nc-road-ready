## PDF page 6 — printed page 4 — DMV Website, Duplicate Cards, Online Renewal

[Official PDF, page 6](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=6).

The NCDMV website provides DMV information, office directories, legislative or requirement updates, publications, and forms.

A duplicate driver license or ID card may be requested online if DMV has the customer's most recent image on file and the customer has a valid Social Security number or a U.S. government document showing legal presence. Online duplicates are available to replace a lost or stolen card, update a residence or mailing address, or replace a damaged or defaced card. The duplicate fee is $16.75, payable by credit or debit card. The duplicate uses the most recent photo in the computer system and is mailed to the customer.

Online driver license renewal is available every other renewal cycle and may be done up to six months before expiration. The driver must verify identity, confirm residence at the address on the license, state that no vision problem hinders driving, attest to truthful statements, provide the current driver license number, and pay with a major credit or debit card. The new license is mailed in 7-14 business days and is valid for either five or eight years depending on the driver's age.

The online system is not available for first-time licenses, ID cards, or first-time REAL ID issuances. It is also unavailable when the driver has a restriction other than corrective lenses, is in medical review, is renewing a CDL or state ID card, or has an expired, suspended, or revoked license.

## PDF page 10 — printed page 8 — License Requirement, Office Hours, Learner Permits

[Official PDF, page 10](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=10).

Driving is described as a legal privilege and responsibility. It is illegal to drive on streets or highways without a valid driver license. The handbook also says it is illegal without a valid license to sit in the driver's seat while the engine is running or to steer a motor vehicle while it is pushed or towed by another vehicle.

Most driver license offices are open 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday, though smaller-town offices may operate only on certain days. Appointments can be made online at skiptheline.ncdot.gov, but the handbook says appointments are not required and walk-ins are accepted all day or until capacity is reached. Applicants for an original license may be tested on vision, motor vehicle law knowledge, traffic signs, and on-road driving skill.

Learner permits are available to people age 18 and older. A learner permit authorizes driving a specified type or class of vehicle, but the permit holder must carry the permit and be accompanied by a licensed person for that vehicle type seated beside the permit holder. Required learner permit tests include vision, traffic signs, and motor vehicle law knowledge.

For a motorcycle learner permit, applicants age 16 to under 18 must already have a full provisional license and a parent or guardian signature; applicants age 18 or older must have a DMV-issued license. The motorcycle learner permit allows motorcycle operation without passengers. Applicants under 18 must complete either the Motorcycle Safety Foundation Basic Rider Course or the North Carolina Motorcycle Safety Education Program Basic Rider Course.

## PDF page 11 — printed page 9 — Motorcycle Permit, Test Requirements

[Official PDF, page 11](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=11).

A motorcycle learner permit is valid for 12 months and may be renewed once for an additional six months. Required testing includes vision, traffic signs, and the motorcycle knowledge test. Each driver license test must be passed separately. CDL applicants are directed to the CDL Handbook; motorcycle endorsement and motorcycle learner permit applicants are directed to the Motorcycle Handbook.

For in-person learner permit, original license, or renewal applications, vision is checked against DMV standards. A driver who needs corrective lenses to meet the standards must wear them whenever driving; the license will show a corrective-lenses restriction. Driving without required corrective lenses carries the same penalty as driving without a license.

The traffic signs test requires applicants to identify traffic signs by color and shape and explain their meanings. The knowledge test covers traffic laws and safe driving practices; audio tests are available on request for people with reading difficulty.

The driving skills test is an on-road demonstration of driving ability after the other tests are passed. First-time license applicants must take it, while learner permit applicants do not. A renewal applicant may also be required to take it. Health rules allow DMV to deny licensing for mental or physical conditions that would prevent safe driving, while a person with a disability may receive a restricted license if safe driving remains possible.

## PDF page 12 — printed page 10 — Road Test Skills and License Classes

[Official PDF, page 12](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=12).

The on-road test may grade intersection approaches, stop signs, traffic signals, quick stops, backing, stopping, starting, parking, clutch use in manual-transmission vehicles, turn signals, horn use, turning, lane use, following, passing, being passed, yielding to pedestrians and vehicles, driving posture, and a three-point turnabout. The three-point turnabout sidebar describes starting from the right edge, signaling left and moving forward while turning left, backing while turning right, then moving forward while turning left to complete the turn.

North Carolina has regular driver licenses and commercial driver licenses. The vehicle operated determines the license class and the vehicle used for the driving skills test; the skills test vehicle must represent the desired class.

Regular license classes are:

| Class | Handbook description |
|---|---|
| A | Required for a non-CDL-exempt combination when the towed unit has a GVWR of 10,001 pounds or more. |
| B | Required for a non-CDL-exempt single vehicle with GVWR of 26,001 pounds or more, and such a vehicle towing a vehicle with GVWR not over 10,000 pounds. |
| C | Required for a noncommercial single vehicle with GVWR under 26,001 pounds, and for a vehicle towing another vehicle with combined GVWR under 26,001 pounds when operated by a driver age 18 or older. Most drivers need only Regular Class C for personal cars and small trucks. |

## PDF page 13 — printed page 11 — CDL and Endorsements

[Official PDF, page 13](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=13).

A CDL is required for paid or volunteer drivers who operate certain passenger or property vehicles. CDL Class A covers a combination with combined GVWR of at least 26,001 pounds that includes a towed unit with GVWR of at least 10,001 pounds. CDL Class B covers either a single vehicle with GVWR of at least 26,001 pounds or a combination with a towing unit of at least 26,001 pounds and a towed unit under 10,001 pounds. CDL Class C covers vehicles outside Class A or B that either are designed to transport 16 or more passengers including the driver, or transport placarded hazardous materials.

The handbook says a CDL is not required for recreational vehicles, military equipment, fire or emergency equipment, or certain farm vehicles, but a regular license of the appropriate class is still required. Volunteer fire, rescue, or EMS members performing duty may operate Class A, B, or C emergency vehicles or combinations while holding a Regular A, B, or C license.

Special CDL endorsements are required to haul hazardous materials, transport passengers, drive school buses and school activity buses, pull double trailers, or drive tank vehicles. A motorcycle requires either a regular or commercial license with motorcycle endorsement or a motorcycle learner permit.

School bus drivers must be at least 18, have at least six months of driving experience, hold a Class B or C CDL with both S and P endorsements, and hold a School Bus Driver's Certificate after special training and examination. School activity bus drivers have the same requirements except the School Bus Driver's Certificate is not required. The handbook says DMV may not issue or renew a CDL showing P or S endorsement to a person required to register under sex offender and public protection registration programs.

## PDF page 14 — printed page 12 — Graduated Licensing, Level One

[Official PDF, page 14](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=14).

For a Level One Limited Learner Permit, the driver must be at least 15 but under 18, reside in North Carolina, and have completed an approved driver education course. The course must include at least 30 classroom hours and six behind-the-wheel hours. The applicant must pass written, sign, and vision tests.

During the first six months, a Level One permit authorizes driving only from 5 a.m. to 9 p.m. with a supervising driver. Six months after Level One issuance, the permit holder may drive at any time with a supervising driver. The permit must be held for nine months before applying for a Limited Provisional License.

The permit holder receives a driving log documenting at least 60 hours of operation, including at least 10 nighttime hours. Daytime driving is from sunrise to sunset and while daylight exists; nighttime driving is after sunset and during hours without sunlight or daylight. The supervising driver must sign the log, and the log must be submitted when applying for the Limited Provisional License.

All passengers must use seat belts or child safety seats. Only the driver and supervising driver may sit in the front seat. Cell phone or associated technology use is prohibited while operating on a public street, highway, or public vehicular area, except to call emergency response, hospital/physician/clinic, ambulance, fire department, law enforcement, or a parent, legal guardian, or spouse during an emergency. To graduate to Level Two, the driver must have no motor vehicle moving violation, seat belt, or cell phone infraction convictions in the preceding six months. A supervising driver must be a parent, grandparent, guardian, or parent-approved responsible person, hold a valid license, and have been licensed for at least five years.

## PDF page 15 — printed page 13 — Level Two and Level Three Provisional Licenses

[Official PDF, page 15](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=15).

A Level Two Limited Provisional License is for drivers at least 16 but under 18. Unsupervised driving is allowed from 5 a.m. to 9 p.m. and at any time when driving directly to or from work or, for members, volunteer fire, rescue, or emergency medical service. The license must be held at least six months before applying for a Full Provisional License. Passengers must be restrained, and when a supervising driver is used that person must sit beside the driver.

When driving unsupervised, the driver may have one passenger under 21 who is not a household member. One additional under-21 non-household passenger is allowed when that passenger is a student and is being driven directly to or from school. There is no under-21 passenger limit if all passengers are members of the driver's household. When unsupervised, under-21 household passengers may not be in the vehicle at the same time as under-21 non-household passengers. Cell phone and associated technology use is prohibited with the same emergency-call exceptions listed for Level One. Graduation to Level Three requires no moving violation, seat belt, or cell phone infraction convictions in the preceding six months. The supervising driver definition remains the same as Level One.

For Level Three Full Provisional License, eligible drivers may upgrade from Level Two online. At age 18, eligible drivers may upgrade online to a regular Class C license. Level One and Two restrictions on driving time, supervision, and passengers do not apply. A driver under 18 still may not use a cell phone or associated technology while operating a vehicle on public roads or public vehicular areas, except for the listed emergency contacts.

## PDF page 16 — printed page 14 — New Residents Age 15-18 and Provisional Licensee Rules

[Official PDF, page 16](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=16).

New North Carolina residents age 15 to under 18 who have a learner permit, restricted license, or unrestricted license are directed to contact a local driver license office to determine the license or permit they may apply for.

Drivers under 18 are provisional licensees and are subject to special laws because of a higher crash rate. They must present proof of an approved driver education course with at least 30 classroom hours and six behind-the-wheel hours before testing for a learner permit or license. DMV reviews their driving records more closely and may contact them after certain traffic convictions or crashes.

A provisional licensee may not drive after or while consuming any amount of alcohol or drugs; conviction leads to a one-year revocation. A parent or legal guardian must sign for a minor. Provisional licensees may not use a cellphone and may not operate a vehicle while manually entering multiple letters or text for communication or while reading email or text messages. Everyone in a moving vehicle must have a safety belt properly fastened.

## PDF page 17 — printed page 15 — School Discipline, Restrictions, Required Documents Intro

[Official PDF, page 17](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=17).

The "Lose Control/Lose Your License" rule says license loss occurs when a student receives either a suspension for more than 10 consecutive days or an assignment to an alternative educational setting for more than 10 consecutive days because of disciplinary action. The suspension remains effective for 12 months.

Driver licenses may be restricted for safety. Examples include a corrective-lenses restriction and a restriction requiring equipment needed for safe operation. Driving outside the limits of a restriction is treated as driving without a license.

For a first North Carolina driver license, learner permit, or ID card, applicants must provide proof of full name and date of birth, North Carolina residence address, Social Security number or documentation indicating legal presence, and, for driver licenses, proof of vehicle liability insurance. DMV may copy presented documents or hold them briefly to verify authenticity. Altered documents are not accepted. The page cites North Carolina General Statutes 20-7 and 20-11 and 6 CFR Part 37.

The handbook says cards marked "Not for Federal Identification" remain valid state-issued driver licenses, permits, or ID cards. Beginning May 7, 2025, that marking means additional documentation is needed to fly on a commercial airline and enter federal facilities, military bases, and nuclear power plants that require identification; visitors should contact the facility for required documentation. Applicants under 18 must also provide proof of driver education: 30 classroom hours and six behind-the-wheel hours.

## PDF page 23 — printed page 21 — Proof of Legal Presence

## PDF page 33 — printed page 31 — Alcohol and DWI Basics

[Official PDF, page 33](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=33).

The handbook states that everyone's driving is impaired at BAC 0.08 percent, while many people are affected at lower levels. It says crash involvement risk increases at 0.05 percent and fatal-crash causation risk increases further at 0.08 percent.

North Carolina's 1983 Safe Roads Act replaced prior drunk-driving laws with the single offense of Driving While Impaired, or DWI. If charged with DWI, the driver will be asked to take a breath or blood chemical test. Refusing a required test causes immediate license revocation for at least 30 days and an additional DMV revocation of at least 12 months. In some cases, after six months of the willful-refusal revocation, a judge may issue a limited driving privilege.

If a chemical test shows BAC 0.08 or more, or 0.04 or more while driving a commercial motor vehicle, the driving privilege is revoked immediately for at least 30 days. Test results or refusal may be admitted as court evidence.

DWI can be proven either by showing appreciable impairment of physical or mental fitness by alcohol, drugs, or both, or by showing BAC of 0.08 or more, or 0.04 or more for a commercial motor vehicle driver. DWI laws allow impaired-driver checkpoints, prohibit consuming alcoholic beverages while driving, prohibit transporting an open container of alcoholic beverage in the passenger area, prohibit possession of alcoholic beverages in the passenger area of a commercial motor vehicle on a highway, street, or public vehicular area, and create punishment levels based on offense severity. They also require second-conviction DWI offenders to serve jail time.

## PDF page 42 — printed page 40 — Chapter 4 opening; driver condition and drowsy driving

[Official PDF, page 42](https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/license-id/driver-licenses/new-drivers/Documents/nc-driver-handbook.pdf#page=42).

Chapter 4 frames safe driving as practiced skill plus awareness of physical and mental limits. Drivers should not drive while too tired, sleepy, ill, or less alert than normal. Moped riding practices:

- Stay out of traffic as much as possible and choose routes that are less heavily traveled.
- Keep reasonable space from other vehicles.
- Keep to the right side of the lane and make as much space as possible for faster traffic to pass.

- Wear light or bright clothing so other drivers can see you.

Littering:

- Littering in North Carolina is illegal and harmful to people, wildlife, and the environment.
- Roadside litter can attract wildlife to road edges and increase wildlife-vehicle crash risk.
- When litter is thrown or blown from a vehicle, the vehicle’s driver is responsible.
- A first conviction carries a fine of at least $250 and at most $1,000.
- A second or later offense within three years of a prior offense carries a fine of at least $500 and at most $2,000.

Adopt-A-Highway:

- A participating civic, business, social, or family group agrees to pick up litter at least four times per year along a two-mile section of state-maintained road.
- NCDOT installs two signs identifying the adopted roadside section and recognizing the group.
- NCDOT provides safety vests and training.
- Groups are asked to recycle as much collected litter as possible.
- Contact information: NCDOT Office of Beautification website at www.ncdot.gov/beautification; phone 919-707-2970.

Vehicle registration:

- A North Carolina driver license is required before titling or registering a vehicle in the state.
- Except for exempt farm vehicles and exempt road-construction equipment, every vehicle owner must register the motor vehicle with DMV.
- A new North Carolina resident moving from out of state must use a valid out-of-state driver license together with the North Carolina Temporary Driving Certificate to title and register a vehicle.
- NCDMV.gov is the source listed for title and registration information, online services, fees, forms, and license plate office hours and locations.

To register a vehicle:

- The owner must complete the required application forms.
- The owner must pay the appropriate title and registration fees.

After DMV receives the registration application:

- DMV issues a registration card, license plate, and validation stickers.
- The registration card must stay in the vehicle and be available to law enforcement on request.
- The plate displays the vehicle’s assigned number, the registration expiration date, and North Carolina registration status.
- The plate must be displayed as required by law.

Vehicle registration address changes:

- A physical address change for vehicle registration can be submitted online through the duplicate vehicle registration card application at NCDMV.gov.
- Required information includes the license plate number, the last five characters of the title number, and a valid email address for the receipt.
- There is no charge for changing an address online.
- If the registration is expired, the owner must renew and change the address at the same time and pay the regular renewal fee.

Name and address changes:

Proofs and documents for vehicle registration:

- To register a used vehicle for the first time in North Carolina, the applicant must present the title from the former state of registration.

Continuous coverage:

- If DMV is notified that an owner is operating without proper liability insurance, the license plate may be revoked for 30 days unless proof of continuous coverage is provided.
- If a lapse occurred, the registered owner may face a fine or penalty.
- Every registered vehicle owner must maintain continuous liability insurance while the vehicle has a valid license plate.
- Liability policies are effective at 12:01 a.m. on the issuance date, last six months, and expire at 12:01 a.m. six months later.
- North Carolina does not honor an insurance grace period. Any grace period is only between the insured and the insurance company.
- Additional information is available on the DMV website or by calling 919-715-7000.

Vehicle registration renewal:

- Within 90 days before registration expiration, every self-propelled vehicle must receive the required safety inspection or safety-and-emissions inspection to renew registration.
- When registration expires, the owner renews by submitting a renewal application and paying the registration fee.
- After payment, DMV issues plate stickers indicating the new registration and inspection period.
- DMV uses staggered registration renewals across all 12 months of the year.
- To avoid fines or penalties, a vehicle’s valid license plate should be surrendered before liability insurance is terminated or cancelled.

Transfer of a motor vehicle:

- A vehicle owner transferring ownership should use section A on side two of the certificate of title.
- For the transfer to be valid, reassignment must occur in the presence of a Notary Public.
- Actual delivery of the vehicle to the new owner must occur.
- If a registration plate will be requested, a current inspection from within the 12 months before sale is required.

Transfer of license plates:

- Plates may be transferred from one vehicle to another vehicle of the same category when ownership is in the same name and insurance coverage has been continuous.

Replacement title or registration card:

- A lost or stolen certificate of title can be replaced by completing a duplicate-title application and paying the fee.
- DMV cannot issue the duplicate title until 15 days after it receives the application and fee.
- Duplicate-title forms are available at local DMV vehicle registration offices and at NCDMV.gov.
- A duplicate registration card can be obtained by paying the required fee, with forms available at local registration offices and on the DMV website.

Tag & Tax Together:

- North Carolina requires vehicle registration renewal and vehicle property taxes to be paid at the same time.
- Both the tag renewal and tax payment must be paid in full to renew annual registration.
- Annual combined notices are mailed to current vehicle owners and include registration, inspection, and tax information.
- DMV does not determine vehicle value for property taxes. The local county assessment office determines tax based on the make and model.

Vehicles not covered by Tag & Tax Together:

- Vehicles exempt from registration by North Carolina law.
- Manufactured homes.
- Mobile classrooms and offices.
- Semi-trailers registered on a multi-year basis.
- Motor vehicles owned or leased by public service companies.
- Park model trailers.
- Mopeds.

Vehicle emissions and safety inspection:

- DMV License & Theft Bureau oversees vehicle inspections.

- A vehicle must pass the required safety-only inspection or safety-and-emissions inspection before it can be registered and before the license tag can be renewed.
- Inspections can be performed at any licensed inspection station in the state. NCDMV.gov is listed for current station locations.
- Inspections may be completed up to 90 days before the registration/license-tag expiration date.
- A vehicle that fails inspection may be reinspected at the same station within 60 days without paying another inspection fee.

Emissions inspection exemption:

- A light-duty gasoline-powered vehicle that is three years old or newer and registered in one of the 19 emissions counties may be exempt from the emissions inspection under G.S. 20-183.2.
- Vehicles with less than 70,000 miles and within the three most recent model years receive only a safety inspection.
- The vehicle model year appears on the registration card under "YEAR."
